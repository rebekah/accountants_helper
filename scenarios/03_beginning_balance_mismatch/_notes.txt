SCENARIO 03 — BEGINNING BALANCE MISMATCH
=========================================
Edge case: The Schedule L beginning-of-year RE for Year 2 does not match the
Schedule L end-of-year RE from Year 1. The books are unaffected.

Why this happens (common causes):
  - An amended return (1120-X) was filed for Year 1 AFTER Year 2's return was prepared,
    changing the prior-year balance sheet without updating Year 2's beginning figures.
  - An IRS examination adjustment to Year 1 was accepted but not reflected in Year 2.
  - A data-entry error in the tax software when rolling the prior year's ending balances.

Specific mismatch in this scenario:
  Year 1 (2023) Schedule L ending RE:    $220,000
  Year 2 (2024) Schedule L beginning RE: $205,000  ← $15,000 lower (unexplained)

  Implied cause: accumulated depreciation on the amended return was $15,000 higher
  than originally reported, reducing retained earnings by $15,000.

The 2023 ledger and Schedule L are IDENTICAL to Scenario 01 (no issue in Year 1).
The 2024 ledger is also IDENTICAL to Scenario 01 (books are unaffected by the mismatch).
Only the 2024 Schedule L has different beginning balances.

Key numbers:
  2023 Schedule L ending RE:      $220,000
  2024 Schedule L beginning RE:   $205,000  ← MISMATCH — flag this
  2024 Schedule L ending RE:      $321,000

What the software should do:
  - Compare prior-year Schedule L end_of_year RE with current-year beginning_of_year RE.
  - Raise an error/warning if they differ: "Prior year Schedule L closing RE ($220,000)
    does not match current year Schedule L opening RE ($205,000). Difference: $15,000.
    Possible causes: amended return, audit adjustment, or data entry error.
    Provide a reconciling explanation before proceeding."
  - Do NOT silently use either number — require the user to choose or explain.
