SCENARIO 02 — TAX ADJUSTMENTS NOT BOOKED BACK TO THE GL
=========================================================
Edge case: The tax accountant made adjusting entries on the return that were never
posted back to the company's general ledger. This is extremely common in practice —
the client's bookkeeper keeps the books, the tax preparer adjusts the trial balance
for the return, and those adjustments stay only in the tax software.

Specific adjustments made to the 2023 return (not in books):
  1. Switched depreciation from straight-line ($10k/yr) to MACRS ($20k yr-1):
       Extra depreciation on return: $10,000 → reduces taxable income by $10,000
  2. Capitalized $5,000 repair expense as a leasehold improvement:
       Removes $5,000 from expenses on return → increases taxable income by $5,000
  Net impact on return: ($5,000) vs. books — tax income $5,000 lower than book income

  Additional MACRS depreciation in 2024: $8,000 more than book SL depreciation
  No new capitalizations in 2024.

Key numbers:
  2023 book ending RE:          $220,000  (same as Scenario 01)
  2023 Schedule L ending RE:    $215,000  ← $5,000 lower due to tax adjustments
  2024 book ending RE:          $336,000  (same as Scenario 01)
  2024 Schedule L ending RE:    $323,000  ← cumulative $13,000 gap

Balance sheet differences (Schedule L vs. books):
  Equipment gross:   +$5,000  (capitalized repair added to asset)
  Accumulated depr:  higher   (more MACRS depreciation taken)
  Net assets:        lower than books (MACRS depr > extra asset capitalized)

The LEDGER files in this scenario are IDENTICAL to Scenario 01.
Only the Schedule L files differ.

What the software should do:
  - Detect that Schedule L ending RE ≠ book ending RE in 2023.
  - Warn that a book-tax difference exists and request a reconciliation note.
  - Ask whether to roll the BOOK balance or the SCHEDULE L balance into next year.
  - Track the cumulative book-tax difference so it can be reported.
